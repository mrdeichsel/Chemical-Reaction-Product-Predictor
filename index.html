Reaction Prediction Reference Guide

Diatomic elements: H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ • Common polyatomic ions: NO₃⁻, OH⁻, SO₄²⁻, CO₃²⁻, PO₄³⁻

1. Double Displacement
AB + CD → AD + CB

Positive ions (cations) swap negative ions (anions). Balance charges using the criss-cross method. Acid + Base always forms a Salt + Water (H₂O).

2. Single Displacement
A + BC → AC + B

A reactive metal replaces the cation (or hydrogen in acids/water). A reactive halogen replaces the halide anion. Watch out for diatomic elements!

3. Complete Combustion
CₓHᵧ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

Any hydrocarbon or alcohol reacting completely with oxygen (O₂) always produces carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water (H₂O).

4. Carbonate Decomposition
MCO₃ → MO + CO₂

When heated, a metal carbonate breaks down into a metal oxide (charge-balanced with O²⁻) and carbon dioxide gas (CO₂).

5. Metal Oxide + Water Synthesis
MO + H₂O → M(OH)ₓ

A metal oxide reacts with water to synthesize a single basic product: a metal hydroxide (balance the metal cation with OH⁻).

6. Binary Compound Decomposition
AB → A + B

A binary compound (made of two elements) breaks down into its two pure elements. Remember to apply subscript 2 to any of the 7 diatomic elements!

7. Binary Ionic Synthesis
M + X → MₐXᵦ

A metallic element and a nonmetallic element combine into a single binary ionic compound. Criss-cross cation and anion charges—do not copy diatomic subscripts!

8. Nonmetal Oxide + Water Synthesis
XO + H₂O → HₐXOᵦ

A nonmetal oxide (acid anhydride) reacts with water to synthesize a single acidic product: an oxyacid (keep the nonmetal's oxidation state unchanged).
